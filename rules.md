# Hand-Foot-Toe Rules

Point values and meld requirements below are the defaults. If they are changed in the app's Settings, the app's Rules tab shows the current values.

## Overview

- Four players in two partnerships. Partners sit across from each other; play goes clockwise.
- Six standard decks, jokers included (324 cards).
- Each player gets three piles: a **hand** (15 cards), a **foot** (13) and a **toe** (11), played in that order.
- A game is four rounds. Each round is scored on its own; the round scores add up to the game score.
- Partners never share cards or information about their cards.

## Cards and values

| Card | Points | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Joker | 50 | Wild |
| 2 | 20 | Wild |
| Ace | 20 | |
| 8 through King | 10 | |
| 4 through 7 | 5 | |
| Black 3 | 5 | Never melded; only blocks the discard pile. Counts only as a penalty. |
| Red 3 | 100 | Bonus when tabled; penalty if still held when the round ends. |

All other cards are **natural** cards.

## Dealing

### Who starts

- Round 1: after the shuffle each player cuts for a card. High card starts. 2 is low, ace beats king, a joker beats everything. Tied players re-cut until there is no tie.
- Rounds 2–4: the start moves one player clockwise each round.

### The deal

1. The starting player cuts a stack from the deck and deals four hands of 15 cards, each a separate stack in front of them.
2. The next player clockwise cuts a stack and deals four feet of 13.
3. The next player clockwise cuts a stack and deals four toes of 11.
4. The fourth player takes a nap.
5. The stacks are handed out to all four players: toes first, then feet, then hands. Foot and toe stay face down and unseen until they are picked up.
6. The rest of the cards are the stock. No card is turned up; the discard pile starts with the first discard.

**Deal bonus (200):** if the hand dealer's cut is exactly 60 cards, or the foot dealer's cut is exactly 52 cards, that dealer's team scores 200. The toe is not eligible. The hand and foot dealers are on opposite teams, so a team can earn at most one deal bonus per round.

## A turn

1. **Red threes in hand.** At the start of the turn, before drawing, the player may table red threes they are holding (including any dealt to them) and draw two replacement cards from the stock for each one. This is the only time red threes already in hand can be tabled.
2. **Draw.** Either draw two cards from the stock, or take the discard pile (see *Taking the discard pile*).
   - A red three drawn may be tabled immediately and replaced with two more cards from the stock. Every further red three drawn may be replaced the same way.
   - A red three does not have to be tabled. One still held when someone goes out is a penalty.
3. **Play.** Lay down melds, add to melds, complete canastas.
4. **Discard** one card. A discard is required to end every turn except the one in which the player goes out.

The player who started the deal plays first. If the stock runs out, shuffle the discard pile to make a new stock.

## The initial meld

Before a team can put cards on the table, one partner must lay down melds worth at least the team's meld requirement, in a single turn, using only their own cards. After that, both partners may play freely.

| Team score at start of round | Meld needed |
|---:|---:|
| 0 – 7,499 | 50 |
| 7,500 – 14,999 | 90 |
| 15,000 – 22,499 | 120 |
| 22,500 – 29,999 | 150 |
| 30,000 – 37,499 | 180 |
| 37,500 + | 220 |

- The requirement comes from the team's game score after the previous rounds. Round 1 always needs 50; a negative score also needs 50.
- Only melded cards count, at their card values. Red threes, the deal bonus and discards count for nothing.
- A canasta laid down as part of the initial meld counts only its card values, not its canasta bonus.
- The partner cannot contribute cards or help.

## Melds

- A meld is at least 3 cards of the same rank: 3 naturals, or 2 naturals and 1 wild.
- Naturals must outnumber wilds in a meld at all times (4 naturals + 3 wilds at most in 7 cards). The one exception is a meld made entirely of wild cards, which builds toward a wild canasta.
- Threes are never melded. Red threes are tabled as bonuses; black threes are never played.
- A team may have only one open meld of any rank at a time. A new meld of that rank can be started only after the open one becomes a canasta and moves to the completed board (immediately, if the player has the cards). So a dirtied meld of sevens must be completed as a dirty canasta before a new, possibly clean, meld of sevens can be started.
- Usual practice: one partner keeps the **meld board** (melds still being built), the other keeps the **completed board** (finished canastas and red threes).

## Canastas

A meld becomes a canasta when it reaches exactly 7 cards, and it moves to the completed board.

| Canasta | Made of | Bonus |
|---|---|---:|
| Clean | 7 naturals | 500 |
| Dirty | 7 cards including wilds; naturals outnumber wilds | 300 |
| Sevens | 7 natural sevens | 2,000 |
| Fives | 7 natural fives | 1,500 |
| Wild | 7 wild cards, any mix of jokers and 2s | 1,000 |

### Base and regular canastas

- The completed board has a **base**: one clean, one dirty, one sevens, one fives and one wild canasta. The base is what a team needs to go out.
- Further sevens, fives or wild canastas also go in the base (two sevens canastas, for example).
- Further clean and dirty canastas are **regular** canastas.
- Sevens or fives that include wild cards make an ordinary dirty canasta. It is a regular canasta, scores as dirty, and does not count as the base sevens or fives.

### Adding to canastas

- Base canastas are closed; nothing may be added to them.
- A regular canasta may have natural cards of its own rank added to it.
- Wild cards may never be added to any canasta.
- The player who completes a canasta may not add to it until their next turn.

## Taking the discard pile

- Taking the pile means taking the top 7 cards, or the whole pile if it holds fewer than 7.
- The player must hold two natural cards matching the top card and play them with it immediately, either as a new meld or added to an existing meld of 3 or 4 cards. A meld of 5 or 6 cannot take three more cards, so it cannot be used.
- The pile is **blocked** while the top card is a black three, a red three or a wild card. It stays blocked until another card is discarded on top.
- **Before the team has melded:** the pile may be taken only if the top card, together with cards in the player's hand, makes the initial meld. The player takes the top card, lays down the meld, and only then takes the other six cards.

## Foot and toe

### The foot (picked up by the team)

- A team may pick up its feet once it has completed its first canasta.
- The player who completed the canasta does not pick up during that turn. The partner may look at their foot right away but cannot play it until their own turn.
- When the player who completed the canasta finishes their turn, both partners pick up their feet; each foot joins the cards left in that player's hand.
- A player can empty their hand only by discarding its last card; the last card of the hand may never be melded.

### The toe (picked up by each player)

- A player picks up their toe as soon as they have played every card from their hand and foot.
- If that happens during play, they pick up the toe immediately and keep playing.
- If their discard empties their hand, they pick up the toe at the start of their next turn.

## Going out

- To go out, the team's base must be complete: at least one clean, one dirty, one sevens, one fives and one wild canasta.
- The player going out must be playing from their toe and must ask their partner's permission first.
- Until going out, a player must always be able to discard one card and still keep at least one card.
- The player goes out by getting rid of every card. The last card may be played or discarded; no discard is required.
- The round ends at once. Every card still held by anyone, including feet and toes not yet reached, counts against that player's team.

## Scoring a round

| Item | Points |
|---|---:|
| Deal bonus | 200 |
| Clean canasta | 500 each |
| Dirty canasta | 300 each |
| Sevens canasta | 2,000 each |
| Fives canasta | 1,500 each |
| Wild canasta | 1,000 each |
| Red three tabled | 100 each |
| Going out | 500 |
| Cards on the table (melds and canastas) | plus card values |
| Cards not played: in hand, unreached foot and toe, held red threes | minus card values |

Canasta bonuses and the card count are separate: a canasta's cards are also counted at face value.

## Winning

- After four rounds the team with the higher game score wins. A tie stands.
- A team that is behind may forfeit. The round in progress is scored as it stands, and the leading team wins.
